# Student safety and data protection protocol

**Checked:** 2026-10-05, at David's request, against children's data protection and
online-safety law current on that date. Law in this area is changing fast; re-check
at least once a year. This is guidance for facilitators, not legal advice: follow
your school's or district's policy and local law first.

## The protocol

1. **The site collects nothing.** No accounts, analytics, cookies, or forms that
   send data, and no third-party requests: fonts and the claims engine are hosted
   with the site, and a strict Content Security Policy in every page blocks
   anything else (see `docs/security-and-privacy-checks.md`). It remembers only the
   theme and audience track, in the learner's own browser. GitHub, which hosts the
   site, logs visitors' IP addresses for security, as its documentation states.
   Each footer says all of this. An organisation that wants no third party at all
   can host the site itself; it is a folder of static files.
2. **Students use only AI tools the school approves,** within each tool's own age
   limits and local law. UNESCO recommends 13 as the minimum age for generative AI
   in classrooms. Many tools set 13 (with parental consent) or 18. Every students
   "Use it this week" task that involves an AI tool or app says this.
3. **Students never type personal details into AI tools:** name, address,
   photos, location, contacts, health or school records. Nothing in the course
   asks them to.
4. **No student shares personal stories in class.** Warm-ups and activities use
   made-up scenarios, show of hands, or private reflection. No scenario asks a
   student to judge a classmate, friend or family member.
5. **Safeguarding comes first in Lessons 10–12.** Know your route before teaching:
   in England, the designated safeguarding lead (KCSIE 2026); in the US,
   mandated-reporting duties under state law. Never promise a child secrecy. A
   learner-visible note opens each of these lessons.
6. **Help routes are names and links only,** never a form on this site.
7. **Reporting problems with the course** goes through GitHub, which is for ages
   13 and up. Under-18s ask a teacher to report it for them, and the Sources page
   says so.

## The law, by place (checked 2026-10-05)

| Where | Law or guidance | What it means for this course |
|---|---|---|
| US | FTC, amended COPPA Rule: effective 23 June 2025, full compliance from 22 April 2026. Tighter consent for under-13s' data, including separate consent before disclosure to third parties. | The site collects no personal information, so it is outside COPPA's collection rules. Students under 13 should only use AI tools that a school has approved and that meet COPPA. |
| US | Kids Internet and Digital Safety (KIDS) Act, bundling the Kids Online Safety Act and "COPPA 2.0": passed the House 29 June 2026; before the Senate. | Not law yet. Re-check its status. |
| US states | At least eight states have enacted social media bans or parental-consent rules for minors; several are blocked or paused in court. | No activity requires a student to use social media. |
| US | Mandated reporting: every state requires certain people, including teachers, to report suspected child abuse or neglect. | Lesson 12's educators track and the facilitator notes. |
| UK | Online Safety Act 2023: Ofcom's Protection of Children Codes in force from 25 July 2025. | Applies to platforms, not this course; Lessons 10–12 teach the risks it addresses. |
| UK | ICO, Age Appropriate Design Code (Children's Code): 15 standards, including geolocation off by default. | Supports Lesson 8's students key ("stop" for an app that asks for location). |
| England | Department for Education, Keeping Children Safe in Education 2026. | Lesson 12's disclosure situation and the facilitator notes. |
| EU | AI Act, Article 5(1)(b): bans AI that exploits vulnerabilities due to age; applies from 2 February 2025. | Supports Lesson 11's students key (an AI app that pushes a child to stay or to pay). |
| EU | Digital Services Act, Article 28: Commission guidelines on protecting minors on online platforms, 14 July 2025. | Context for Lessons 7, 10 and 11 (addictive design, grooming, manipulation). |
| EU | GDPR, Article 8: the age at which a child can consent to online services is set by each member state, between 13 and 16. | Another reason students use only school-approved tools. |
| Australia | Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Act 2024: under-16s may not hold accounts on major social media platforms from 10 December 2025, even with parental consent. | No activity requires a student to use social media. |
| International | UNESCO, Guidance for generative AI in education and research (2023): recommends 13 as the minimum age for classroom use. | Protocol point 2. |
