# Recognising exploitative behaviour (the Dark Triad). Series plan

**Status:** direction agreed 2026-09-25; nothing designed beyond draft

**David's direction (2026-09-25):** teach people to recognise behaviour rooted in
the Dark Triad, and to stay away from people who want to exploit their gifts and
talents. Grounded in scientific research. A deep dive as the project continues.

**Decisions (David, 2026-09-25):**

- **Behaviours, not labels.** Name the research construct and its source, but
  teach people to recognise exploitative behaviour and respond to it. Never teach
  them to diagnose a person.
- **All three tracks, students adapted.** The students track centres on
  grooming, peer pressure and online exploitation, routes them to trusted
  adults, and never has them label classmates.

## Why behaviours, not labels (from the research)

- The Dark Triad (narcissism, Machiavellianism, psychopathy) was introduced as
  three **subclinical**, overlapping personality traits in ordinary people
  (Paulhus & Williams, 2002, *Journal of Research in Personality* 36, 556–563).
  They are measured on a scale, not diagnosed.
- The usual research measure is a 27-item self-report questionnaire, the Short
  Dark Triad (Jones & Paulhus, 2014, *Assessment* 21(1), 28–41). It is a
  research tool, not a way to judge someone you know.
- A meta-analysis found the three traits strongly overlap, relate mainly to low
  agreeableness and low honesty-humility, and are linked to a range of negative
  outcomes (Muris et al., 2017, *Perspectives on Psychological Science* 12(2),
  183–204).
- People high in these traits use **varied** influence tactics, which may help
  them avoid detection (Jonason & Webster, 2012, *Personality and Individual
  Differences* 52, 521–526). So a stereotype check misses them, and a check on
  behaviour patterns works better.

All four sources were checked to exist on 2026-09-25. Each finding the lessons
use becomes a `source: "model"` claim for David to attest.

## Draft objectives, first lesson

- D.1 *(understand)*: Describe the three Dark Triad traits as research measures
  on a scale, not diagnoses.
- D.2 *(analyze)*: Tell exploitative patterns apart from ordinary conflict in a
  scenario (for example flattery that turns into demands, taking credit,
  testing boundaries, cutting someone off from others).
- D.3 *(apply)*: Choose a protective response, such as a boundary, a written
  record, or a trusted person or reporting route, without labelling the person.

**The AI link:** exploiters now use AI too: scams, cloned voices, and
"companion" apps built to win trust. That keeps the series inside AI literacy.
